Retro Quest: Seven Cities. Effective November 10, 2026.

Retro Quest is made by one person, Uriah Tobey. On this page "I" and "me" mean me, and "the game" means the Retro Quest app for iPhone, iPad and Android (app ID com.tobeyuj.retroquest). This policy also covers this website.

At a glance

  • There are no accounts and no sign-in.
  • Your save stays on your device. I have no server and can't see it.

  • Credit packs are sold through Apple on iPhone and iPad (section 5). On Android they aren't on sale yet.
  • From version 1.2 the game has optional ads from Google AdMob, which collect ad data (section 4). You only see an ad when you pick WATCH AN AD.
  • I don't sell your information.

1What the game saves on your device

The game keeps a few small files in its private storage on your device:

  • Your game: the name you type for your hero (up to 12 characters, and it doesn't have to be your real name), whether you play as a man or a woman, and your progress, such as your level, items, verses, quests, albums, credits and the game clock.
  • Your settings: volumes, text speed, the switches in Settings such as tap-to-walk, screen shake and the tutorial, and whether you want reminders.
  • A purchase record: transaction IDs, so each purchase adds credits only once.

The game also reads your device's clock and time zone to run its waits and show "ready at" times. That stays on your device too.

Reminders: if you say yes, the game asks your device to show a notification when a trip, rest or post is over and when a daily gift is ready. Your device holds and shows them itself. Nothing about them goes to me or anyone else, and you can turn them off in the game's Settings or your device's settings.

Sharing a post: if you tap SHARE FOR REAL, the game draws a picture of one of your in-game posts (your hero's name, the city, the caption and how many people it reached), saves it in its private storage and opens your device's own share menu. You pick the app and post it yourself. The game never connects to your social media accounts and sends nothing on its own. Whatever you post is handled by that app under its own policy. If you pick Save Image, your device asks you first whether the game may add the picture to your photos.

None of this is sent to me or to anyone else. I can't read it, change it or get it back for you.

2The time check

Waits run on real time, so on phones the game checks the time on the web. It does this when the game starts, when you come back to it, about every 5 minutes while you play, and before a wait ends, a post pays out, a skip is bought or an ad pays its reward.

Each check is one encrypted (HTTPS) request to www.google.com, or to cp.cloudflare.com if Google doesn't answer. The game reads only the time from the reply.

Like any web request, it shows that company your IP address, the game engine's version and your operating system's name. It contains nothing from your save and no device ID. Google and Cloudflare handle what they receive under their own policies: Google's Privacy Policy and Cloudflare's Privacy Policy.

You can still play without a connection, but waits can't finish until the game can check the time.

3What the game doesn't do

  • It has no accounts, sign-in or cloud saves.
  • It has no chat or contact with other players.
  • The phone, posts, followers and photos in the game are pretend. Nothing goes to a real social network, and taking a photo in the game doesn't use your camera.
  • It doesn't ask for your camera, microphone, photo library, contacts or location.
  • It has no analytics or crash-reporting tools of its own.

4Ads

This applies from version 1.2 of the game. Earlier versions have no ad software in them.

Ads are optional. You choose WATCH AN AD in the Credit Shop to earn free credits, and waiting is always free.

Ads come from Google AdMob, with Google's User Messaging Platform for the consent messages. Google's ad software starts when the game opens and runs while it's open, even if you never watch an ad, and it automatically collects and shares:

  • Your IP address, which can estimate your general location.
  • How you use the app and its ads: app launches, taps, video views and the ads you've seen.
  • Diagnostics: crash logs, launch time, hang rate and energy use.
  • Device identifiers: your device's advertising ID and other identifiers tied to the app or its developer.

Google uses this for advertising, analytics and fraud prevention, and encrypts it in transit with TLS. See how Google uses information from apps that use its services and Google's Privacy Policy.

Your choices

  • iPhone and iPad: the game asks before any tracking, using Apple's App Tracking Transparency prompt. If you say no, the game doesn't share your advertising ID. You can change your mind in Settings > Privacy & Security > Tracking.
  • Android: reset or delete your advertising ID in your device's Settings, under Privacy > Ads. The exact path varies by phone.
  • EEA, UK and Switzerland: you'll see Google's consent message before personalized ads. To change your choice later, open Settings in the game and tap ADS (it shows wherever these laws apply).
  • US states with privacy laws: Google's message there lets you opt out of the sale or sharing of your data, and the same ADS button in the game's Settings brings it back.
  • Everywhere: manage ad personalization at Google's My Ad Center.

5Credit packs

Credit packs are on sale in the game on iPhone and iPad. On Android they aren't on sale yet; when they are, this section applies to Google the same way.

Apple or Google handles every purchase. I never see your card, name, email address or store account. The game receives a transaction ID and which pack you bought, and keeps them on your device so each purchase adds credits only once.

Apple and Google send me sales reports for accounting and taxes, with details such as dates, amounts and the general region of each sale. I keep those for as long as tax rules require.

Their policies apply to the purchase itself: Apple's Privacy Policy and Google's Privacy Policy. Refunds go through them too; see Support.

6Apple, Google and TestFlight

Apple and Google run the stores you get the game from, and they collect what their own policies describe. They may show me totals such as downloads, and crash reports from people who agreed to share them with developers. I use these to fix bugs.

If you test the game through TestFlight, Apple also shows me your tester details and any feedback or crash reports you send.

7Who receives data

The only services involved are Google, Cloudflare, Apple and GitHub, which hosts this website. I only use services whose own terms and privacy commitments give your data the same or equal protection as this policy.

I don't sell your information for money. If you allow personalized ads, the ad data in section 4 may count as a "sale" or "sharing" under some US state privacy laws, and the device settings in section 4 are how to opt out.

I'd hand over the little I hold (your emails and sales reports) only if the law requires it.

8How long data is kept and how to delete it

  • On your device: until you delete the app. Deleting it removes your save, settings and credits, including any you bought. NEW GAME replaces your save but keeps your credits. I hold no copies, so I can't restore anything.
  • In your backups: if you back up your device, for example to iCloud, a backup may hold a copy of the game's files until that backup is replaced or deleted.
  • With Google, Cloudflare and Apple: they keep what they receive for as long as their own policies say. Use their privacy tools or contact them directly.
  • With me: I keep emails only as long as I need them to help you, and sales reports as long as tax rules require.

To withdraw consent, change the ad settings in section 4, or delete the app, which stops everything the game collects.

To ask me to delete your data, email retroquest7cities@gmail.com. There's no account to close, and the only things I could hold about you are your emails and sales records. I'll answer within 30 days. I can't delete what Apple, Google or Cloudflare hold, but I'll point you to where to ask.

9Security

The game's files sit in its private storage, which your device's operating system keeps away from other apps. The game doesn't encrypt them itself, so protect your device with a passcode. The time check and Google's ad traffic use encrypted connections.

I run no server and keep no player database, so there's nothing of that kind to breach. Any emails or sales reports I hold stay in password-protected accounts.

10Children

The game has fights and weapons and isn't aimed at children under 13. I don't knowingly collect personal information from anyone under 13. If you're a parent and think your child has sent me personal information, email me and I'll delete it.

11Your rights

Depending on where you live, including the EU, the UK and US states such as California, you may have the right to see the personal data I hold about you, correct it, delete it, get a copy, object to or limit how it's used, and withdraw consent. You may also complain to your local data protection authority. Email me to use any of these. I won't treat you differently for it.

If you're in the EEA or UK, these are my legal bases: legitimate interests for the time check (keeping waits fair) and for answering your email; your consent for ads where the law requires it; and performing our contract for purchases.

Google, Apple, Cloudflare and GitHub may handle data in the United States and other countries.

12This website

This site has no cookies, analytics or ads, and its fonts and images load from the site itself. It's hosted on GitHub Pages, and GitHub logs visitors' IP addresses for security. See GitHub's Privacy Statement.
